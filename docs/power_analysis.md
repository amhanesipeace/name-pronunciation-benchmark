# How small a sample could still detect the gap?

Generated by `power_analysis.py` from the committed scores (gTTS isolated, the clean-baseline run), 1000 subsamples per row, seeded. Each row draws that many names *per group* (English / African) without replacement and re-runs the one-sided Mann-Whitney test; **power** is the fraction that stay significant at p < 0.05.

| Names per group | Power (detection rate) | Median Cliff's delta |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.931 | +0.800 |
| 8 | 0.998 | +0.859 |
| 10 | 0.999 | +0.800 |
| 12 | 1.000 | +0.819 |
| 15 | 1.000 | +0.853 |
| 20 | 1.000 | +0.843 |

- Smallest per-group sample with ≥80% power: **5 names**.
- At 20 names per group the test detects the gap essentially every time (power 1.000).

**Conclusion.** The disparity is large enough that even a handful of names per group detects it reliably, so the study is not *under-powered*. The 20-names-per-language limit is therefore about **representativeness** — whether these names stand in for each language's full name space — not statistical power. That sampling-frame question is the real limitation and is addressed separately (README ethics, `data/DATASHEET.md`), not here.
